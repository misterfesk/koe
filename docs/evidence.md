# Evidence behind the day plan

This is a short, plain summary of the research that shaped how Koe builds a day. It comes from a targeted review of published studies and guidelines, not a systematic review. It is background for design choices. It is not medical advice, and it does not show that Koe itself works. Koe has not been tested in a clinical trial.

## What the design assumes

**The hard part is usually starting, not knowing what to do.** Reviews of what people with depression describe point to low energy, trouble getting going, disconnection and difficulty with everyday self-care such as cooking, cleaning and washing (Fusar-Poli et al., 2023, *World Psychiatry*, doi 10.1002/wps.21111; Haroz et al., 2017, *Social Science & Medicine*, doi 10.1016/j.socscimed.2016.12.030; Eggleston et al., 2025, *Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing*, doi 10.1111/jpm.13155). Measured thinking and concentration difficulties are also common and can persist after other symptoms ease (Rock et al., 2014, *Psychological Medicine*, doi 10.1017/S0033291713002535). So Koe makes every entry small by default and offers a lighter day every day.

**Planning activities is the best-supported idea.** Behavioural activation, which means scheduling activities in a way that fits the person, has been studied in many trials of depression (Cuijpers, van Straten and Warmerdam, 2007, *Clinical Psychology Review*, doi 10.1016/j.cpr.2006.11.001). Most of those trials were small, older and run with a therapist, so the results may overstate what a self-guided app can do. Koe borrows the format and does not promise the result.

**Mix the kinds of activity.** One online study found that doing more activities that gave a sense of achievement went with better mood a week later, while pleasant activities alone did not (Tilden et al., 2020, *Depression Research and Treatment*, doi 10.1155/2020/1387832). Another found that expected pleasure and achievement, rather than the gap between what people expected and what happened, went with improvement (Furukawa et al., 2018, *Journal of Affective Disorders*, doi 10.1016/j.jad.2018.05.067). Both are observational. Koe mixes things you used to enjoy, practical things and basics, and asks how an entry went afterwards.

**Do not assume fun things will work.** Existing treatments change loss of pleasure only a little, and no self-help programme for it has clear trial support in depressed adults (Pugh et al., 2026, *Journal of Affective Disorders*, doi 10.1016/j.jad.2025.120165). Koe never promises enjoyment.

## What Koe leaves out on purpose

- **Weight, calorie, diet or food-tracking content.** Diet research in depression is mixed, mostly in people without clinical depression, and the best-known trial result has been questioned (Firth et al., 2019, *Psychosomatic Medicine*, doi 10.1097/PSY.0000000000000673; Jacka et al., 2017, *BMC Medicine*, doi 10.1186/s12916-017-0791-y; Molendijk et al., 2018, *BMC Medicine*, doi 10.1186/s12916-018-1221-5). In 2023 an eating-disorder helpline took its chatbot offline after users reported weight-loss advice (NPR, 8 June 2023, https://www.npr.org/2023/06/08/1181131532/eating-disorder-helpline-takes-down-chatbot-after-it-gave-weight-loss-advice). Koe can suggest "have something to eat" as basic care and never gives weight, calorie or restriction advice. If someone mentions an eating concern, Koe removes food and exercise suggestions and points to a clinician.
- **Light therapy.** Reviews of bright light for depression that is not seasonal give mixed results, and some report more hypomania (Tuunainen, Kripke and Endo, 2004, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, doi 10.1002/14651858.CD004050.pub2). It is unsuitable for some people, including those with bipolar disorder or some eye conditions, and an app cannot screen for that (Geoffroy et al., 2025, *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience*, doi 10.1080/19585969.2025.2533806). Koe may suggest going outside as an ordinary activity and never presents it as light therapy.
- **Work and employment advice.** Help with work seems to reduce time off sick mainly when combined with clinical care (Nieuwenhuijsen et al., 2020, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, doi 10.1002/14651858.CD006237.pub4). Koe can include one small piece of work or study followed by a break. It does not coach on work decisions.
- **Supplements, medication, diagnoses, streaks and promises of results.**

## Where the evidence is thin

- No trial isolates breaking a task into smaller parts in depression. It is a reasonable format, not a tested ingredient.
- No trial supports "regular meals", hygiene prompts or energy pacing as depression treatments. Koe offers them as ordinary basic care.
- Short breaks reduced tiredness a little in working adults without depression (Albulescu et al., 2022, *PLoS One*, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0272460). That is general-population evidence.

## Limits of this summary

Studies differ in who took part, how care was delivered and how outcomes were measured. Findings from therapist-led trials may not carry over to an app. Absence of evidence here is not proof that something does not help. Koe is a support tool, not a treatment, and it is meant to sit alongside human care.
